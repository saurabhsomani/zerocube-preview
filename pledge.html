Why pledge

Climate action is no longer optional — it is our duty

We already feel it in hotter summers, heavier floods and taps running dry. Each of us adds to the problem, so each of us has a duty to act. Accept that duty once, for life — and one action × many people becomes real change.

  1. ChooseTick the daily habits you will keep, and make the numbers yours.
  2. Commit — once, for lifeOne pledge per person, kept for the rest of your life.
  3. ShareDownload your pledge card and letter; share them with friends.
  4. InspireJoin the Wall of Honour as a Climate Duty Bearer (with your consent) and invite others.

The pledge is free for everyone. Zero Cube members also unlock a formal Commitment Certificate and a Net Zero audit (audit fees as per our fee card; villages never pay).

How we calculate

Each habit uses a published factor and your own small inputs (with cautious defaults). We follow the GHG Protocol idea of comparing what you will do with what you did before, use conservative values and round down. These are indicative estimates for awareness — not measured, verified or certified reductions, and never carbon credits.

  • I will not use the AC more than 3 hours a day, and I will set it to 24–26 °C
    0.75 kWh × (months × 30) × (hours cut + 3 h × 6% × °C raised) × 0.71 Default: Saves about 212 units of electricity a year — ≈150 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will switch off lights and fans when I leave a room, and unplug chargers
    0.04 kWh × hours × 365 × 0.71 Default: Saves about 29 units of electricity a year — ≈20 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will use only LED bulbs and tube-lights at home
    30 kWh × bulbs × 0.71 Default: Saves about 120 units of electricity a year — ≈85 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will dry clothes in the sun, not in a dryer
    2 kWh × loads a week × 52 × 0.71 Default: Nothing extra counted at these settings
  • I will put up rooftop solar on my home
    3 kWh × kW × 365 × 0.71 Default: Makes about 1,095 units of clean electricity a year — ≈777 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will use public transport, or green transport like an electric scooter or car
    km a day × days a week × 50 weeks × net factor (0.08 car→bus · 0.01 two-wheeler→bus or e-scooter · 0 car→EV) Default: 2,500 km a year switched — ≈200 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will walk or cycle for short trips
    km a day × days a week × 50 weeks × 0.10 (car) or 0.02 (two-wheeler) Default: 900 km a year switched — ≈90 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will car-pool to work or college whenever I can
    km × days × 50 weeks × 0.10 × (1 − 1 ÷ people sharing) Default: 3,750 km a year switched — ≈187 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will switch off my engine at long red lights and while waiting
    minutes × days × 50 weeks ÷ 60 × 0.5 L × 2.27 Default: Saves about 12 litres of petrol a year — ≈28 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will eat fresh, local, seasonal food — not processed or packaged food
    packets a week × 52 × 0.008 (packaging only) Default: 364 fewer plastic food packets a year — ≈2 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will cook and take only what we need, and waste no food
    55 kg × people at home × % less waste × 1.0 Default: About 55 kg less food wasted a year — ≈55 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will carry a cloth bag and my own water bottle — no single-use plastic
    bags × 52 × 0.01 + bottles × 52 × 0.05 Default: 416 single-use plastic items avoided a year — ≈10 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will segregate wet and dry waste, and compost kitchen waste
    0.05 kg × people at home × 365 × 0.25 Default: About 73 kg of kitchen waste composted, not dumped — ≈18 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will take a bucket bath and turn off the tap while brushing
    20 L × people at home × 365 ÷ 1,000 × 0.3 Default: Saves about 29,200 litres of water a year — ≈8 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will fix leaking taps and reuse RO and washing water for plants or mopping
    (taps × 10 L + litres reused) × 365 ÷ 1,000 × 0.3 Default: Saves about 5,475 litres of water a year — ≈1 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will buy local, repair before I replace, and reuse what I can
    garments × 4 Default: 4 new garments not bought a year — ≈16 kg CO₂e a year
  • I will plant native trees and care for them for 3 years
    trees × 1 kg a year (young tree, years 1–3) Default: 1 young tree cared for — ≈1 kg CO₂e a year while young (more as it grows)
  • I will teach my family and neighbours one green habit every month
    No CO₂e added (their savings count only if they pledge). Outcome: people reached × 12 habits a year. Default: 4 people reached — 48 green-habit lessons a year (not added to CO₂e)
  • I will help local shops or small businesses save energy and cut waste
    No CO₂e added (depends on the business). Outcome: businesses helped. Default: 1 local business helped to go green (not added to CO₂e)

No double counting: AC, switching off and LEDs each have their own inputs (the old “save 1 unit a day” habit is removed). Travel habits should use different kilometres. Teaching others and helping a shop count people and businesses, not CO₂e. Full factor table and sources →

Why only one pledge, for life?

The Zero Cube Pledge is a promise about who you are, not a yearly target. You take it once, for the rest of your life. We keep one pledge per person (checked by email, and mobile if given) so the Wall of Honour and totals stay honest. You can still ask us to correct or delete your details at any time, as the DPDP Act 2023 allows.

Does Zero Cube collect money for the education pledge?

No. You give support directly to the student or school, or through an NPO project on our Verified Projects listing. The pledge is not a payment or a donation to Zero Cube. Educators pledge their time instead: the students they will personally teach or mentor, and the hours. Educators can also join as an Education Champion, and supporters who fund students as a Student Sponsor (both proposed); membership fees are separate from your support to students.

Is this a donation or a contract?

No. The pledge is a free, voluntary moral commitment and your own responsibility. It is not a legal contract, not a donation, and gives no tax benefit. Membership and donations are separate.

Who sees my pledge?

Only if you tick the Wall of Honour consent and submit, your name, city, pledge and letter appear on the Wall of Honour after a quick review by our team. Your photo appears only with separate photo consent (never for under-18s). Your email is never shown. You can withdraw any time.

Sources for the indicative estimates

  1. Central Electricity Authority (CEA), Government of India — CO2 Baseline Database for the Indian Power Sector (weighted average grid emission factor) (latest version, accessed Oct 2026)
  2. India GHG Program (WRI India, CII, TERI) — India Specific Road Transport Emission Factors (2015)
  3. PIB / Bureau of Energy Efficiency — every 1 °C higher AC setting saves about 6% electricity (2018)
  4. Bureau of Energy Efficiency — Standards & Labelling: room air conditioners (ISEER star ratings) (accessed Oct 2026)
  5. NRDC India — Blueprint for advancing super-efficient ceiling fans in India (typical fan around 70 W; efficient models 35–50 W) (accessed Oct 2026)
  6. Prayas (Energy Group) — Understanding the impacts of India's LED bulb programme, UJALA (realised savings 31–47 kWh per bulb a year) (2018)
  7. Which? — How much does a tumble dryer cost to run (heat-pump dryer about 2.2 kWh per 9 kg load) (accessed Oct 2026)
  8. MNRE — Grid-connected rooftop solar FAQ (1 kW makes 4–5.5 units on a clear sunny day) (2022)
  9. US Department of Energy — Consumer guide to reducing vehicle idling (a car uses about 0.25–0.5 gallon, 0.95–1.9 litres, an hour idling) (2021)
  10. UK DESNZ — Greenhouse gas reporting: conversion factors 2025 (material use, waste disposal, water supply and treatment) (published 10 Jun 2025)
  11. UNEP — Food Waste Index Report 2024 (India household food waste 55 kg per person a year, medium confidence) (Mar 2024)
  12. FAO — Food wastage footprint: impacts on natural resources (3.3 Gt CO2e for 1.3 Gt of food wasted) (2013)
  13. CPCB — Annual Report 2021–22 on the Solid Waste Management Rules, 2016 (all-India average about 0.12 kg waste per person a day) (2022)
  14. IPCC 2006 Guidelines, Vol. 5 Waste, Ch. 3 Solid Waste Disposal (methane correction factor 0.4 for unmanaged shallow sites) (2006 (2019 refinement))
  15. CPHEEO / MoHUA — Manual on Water Supply and Treatment (135 litres per person a day, of which bathing 55 litres) (1999)
  16. US EPA WaterSense — Fix a Leak Week (a tap dripping once a second wastes over 3,000 gallons a year) (accessed Oct 2026)
  17. US Department of Energy — Method for Calculating Carbon Sequestration by Trees in Urban and Suburban Settings, Table 2 (1998)
  18. GHG Protocol — Policy and Action Standard (estimating the effect of actions against a baseline) (2014)
